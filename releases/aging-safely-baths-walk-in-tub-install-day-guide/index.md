Aging Safely Baths has published a free, hour-by-hour guide to walk-in tub installation day, written for older homeowners and the adult children helping them who want to know what actually happens once a crew arrives. The guide follows a typical install in a single-family home from the 7 a.m. arrival to the walkthrough and covers what the crew does, what to have ready, where the surprises hide and what "installed in one day" does and does not include.

A standard walk-in tub or roll-in shower install in an existing alcove takes one working day, roughly 7 a.m. to 4 p.m., with two installers. The morning is protection, removal of the old tub and the plumbing and electrical rough-in. Midday is setting and leveling the new unit and the first pressure test. The afternoon is wall panels, grab bars, fixtures, a 20-minute fill test with the door closed, cleanup and a walkthrough with the bather. About one job in six runs into a second day, usually a half day, because of a large subfloor repair, a water heater or panel upgrade, or a bigger remodel, and the company says it tells customers at the quote when that is likely.

Before the install day, once the customer has chosen a tub or shower, a technician visits the home to take measurements and photos, go over the system and upgrade options, and answer questions. The guide then lists the night-before prep list, the common surprises found behind an old surround with how often each turns up and what each typically costs, separate checklists for condos, manufactured homes, second-floor bathrooms and winter installs, and how lead times run: two to four weeks for stock models and six to eight weeks for custom sizes or colors.

> Families worry about the day more than the tub. They want to know if the bathroom will be torn up for a week and whether something will go wrong. We wrote down exactly how our crews run the day, including the parts that can go sideways, so there are no surprises.

Said by Zach Wennstedt, Founder, Aging Safely Baths.
