What the twelve checks are

Weighted by how much each one affects whether a release gets used.

Structure

Word count, headline length, whether the headline names the company, and whether there is a proper dateline.

Extractability

Specific numbers in the opening, fact density across the first third, and an attributed quote that stands on its own.

Credibility

Stock promotional language, vague quantifiers, and claims that carry no checkable value.

Completeness

Link count and handling, a boilerplate About section, and a media contact somebody actually answers.

About the grader

What does the press release grader check?

Twelve things: word count, headline length, whether the headline names the company, numbers in the opening, fact density in the first third, dateline format, an attributed quote, hype language, unverifiable superlatives, link handling, boilerplate, and a working media contact.

How is the score calculated?

Each check is scored and weighted by how much it affects whether a release gets used. Opening fact density and headline quality carry the most weight, because both editors and extraction systems disproportionately read the top of a document. The scoring is deterministic — the same draft always produces the same score.

What is a good score?

Above 80 is publishable. Between 60 and 80 usually means one or two structural fixes. Below 60 normally means the announcement is buried, vague, or not actually news — and in that last case, no amount of editing helps.

Does the grader use AI?

No. It is deterministic rules running in your browser. That is deliberate: you can rerun it, get the same answer, and check the logic yourself in the page source.

Does my draft get stored anywhere?

No. There is no network request in this tool at all. Your text stays in your browser's memory and disappears when you close the tab.
