What press release distribution costs, and why prices vary so much

You are paying for access to specific channels, not for words. Here is how to tell what a price buys.

It ranges from free, when you publish on your own newsroom, to several hundred dollars or more per release for national wire circuits, before add-ons. The price mostly reflects which channels a release can reach, whether writing is included, and how many extras are billed separately. What you are actually paying for

Two releases with identical text can cost very different amounts, because the text is the cheap part. The price is set by everything around it:

  1. Channel access

    Which places a release can appear. Contract channels, such as newswire circuits that feed financial terminals and subscribing newsrooms, cost the most because the provider maintains those agreements. Open channels, such as public web pages, feeds, search engines and syndication partners, cost far less to reach.

  2. Writing and editing

    Some services only distribute what you send. Others include writing or an editorial review. Writing time is real labor, so check whether it is in the price or billed separately.

  3. Length

    Some rate cards set a word limit and charge for every block of words over it. A release with no word limit removes the temptation to cut facts to stay under a fee.

  4. Media and targeting

    Images, video, logos, extra industry or geographic lists and translations are often priced as add-ons.

  5. Reporting

    A record of where the release appeared. Ask whether it is a list of working links you can click, or a count you cannot check.

Where add-ons hide

The headline price is often the floor. Before you compare two quotes, check each one for:

  • Per-word charges above a set length.
  • Fees per image, video or logo.
  • Extra charges for additional industry or regional lists.
  • Annual membership or account fees on top of the per-release price.
  • Revision fees after you approve a draft.
  • Reporting that costs extra or arrives only as a summary.

None of these is unreasonable on its own. They simply make two prices impossible to compare until you add them up for the release you actually plan to send.

What no price buys

No distribution service can promise that a journalist will write a story, that an outlet will pick up a release, or that a search engine or AI system will cite it. Coverage is an editorial decision made by someone else. Be cautious of any offer that sounds like a guarantee.

Paying for links that pass ranking credit is also off the table. Google's spam policies list "links with optimized anchor text in articles, guest posts, or press releases distributed on other sites" as an example of link spam (Google Search spam policies). That is why every link in our releases carries rel="sponsored" or rel="nofollow". Our guide to press releases and SEO covers what a release still does for search.

How to budget for a year

Start from your news, not from a package. List the genuine announcements you expect over the next twelve months: openings, hires, launches, credentials, events, milestones. That count is your release budget. Buying more releases than you have news tends to produce filler, and filler trains editors and readers to skip your name.

If the list is one or two items, single releases make sense. If it is closer to one a month, a plan usually costs less per release. Either way, put each release on your own newsroom page as well, so the announcement lives somewhere you control.

A note for public companies

If your company has securities disclosure obligations, the channel you use for material news may be governed by those rules. Ask your securities counsel which distribution method satisfies them before choosing on price.

Cost questions

How much does it cost to distribute a press release?

Anywhere from free, if you publish it on your own newsroom, to several hundred dollars or more per release for national wire circuits, before add-ons. Our single release is $99 with writing and all fifteen deliverables included.

Why are some press release services so expensive?

The highest prices usually reflect contract distribution into channels such as newswire circuits, financial terminals and subscribing newsrooms, which the provider maintains through agreements. If your announcement does not need those specific channels, you are paying for reach you will not use.

What hidden fees should I look for?

Per-word charges above a set length, fees per image or video, extra charges for additional industry or regional lists, annual membership fees, revision fees after approval and paid reporting. Add them up for the release you actually plan to send before comparing quotes.

Does paying more guarantee media coverage?

No. No service can guarantee that a journalist writes a story, that an outlet picks up a release or that a search engine or AI system cites it. Coverage is an editorial decision made by someone else.

Is a monthly press release plan worth it?

Only if you have roughly one genuine announcement a month. Count your real news events for the next twelve months first. If the count is low, single releases or a 5-pack used as news happens will cost less.
