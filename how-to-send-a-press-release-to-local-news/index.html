Pitching local news

How to get a local newsroom to read your press release

Local outlets want local stories. Make yours easy to find, easy to check and easy to use.

Start a release See pricing

How do you send a press release to local news?

Confirm the story has a local angle, find the reporter or desk that covers it, and email a short pitch with the release pasted into the body rather than attached. Include a link to the published release, a photo, and a phone number someone answers. Send it with enough lead time, follow up once, and accept that coverage is the newsroom's decision.

Published November 17, 2026. We date these pages because the rules in this field change and undated advice ages badly.

Local coverage is one of the few kinds of press a small business can realistically earn, because local outlets exist to report on their own area. It is still never guaranteed. What you can control is whether the right person sees a clear, checkable story at the right time.

  1. Confirm the local angle

    Ask what a reader in this town gets from the story: new jobs, a new place to go, a change to a street they drive, a neighbor recognized, an event they can attend. If the honest answer is "nothing, but it is good for us," the story belongs on your own website. The free newsworthiness check is a quick way to test it.

  2. Find the right contact

    Sending to a general inbox works sometimes, but a named person who covers your subject works better. Read the outlet for a week: who writes about business, food, schools, real estate? Most newspapers, TV stations and public radio stations publish a contact or "submit a news tip" page. TV and radio usually route stories through an assignment desk. Events often go to a separate community calendar form. Weekly papers, neighborhood newsletters and local business journals are worth a place on the list too.

  3. Publish the release first

    A reporter wants something to check. A link to a permanent public page with the full release, the date and a contact answers that before they ask. It is also where readers land if the story runs. This is the part our service handles: every release is published at its own address, with a newsroom page for your company.

  4. Write a short pitch email

    Put the news in the subject line, plainly: "Golden bakery opens second shop, adding 9 jobs." Open with two or three sentences on what happened and why it matters to local readers. Paste the release text below that, and include the link. Avoid attachments; many newsrooms filter them, and plain text is faster to read on a phone.

  5. Time it

    Send early in the working day so the story can be considered at planning meetings. Events need lead time: community calendars often have their own deadlines, so check each outlet's submission page instead of guessing. If you offer the story before it is public, say so clearly and state the date and time it can be published. That arrangement is called an embargo, and it only works if you honor it yourself.

  6. Have assets ready

    A horizontal photo with a caption and a credit, a spokesperson who can take a call that day, and a phone number that a person answers. Local stories are often turned around quickly. A reporter who cannot reach anyone usually moves to the next story.

  7. Follow up once

    If you hear nothing after a few working days, send one short follow-up that adds something new: a photo, a date, a detail. Then stop. Silence is an answer, and the next announcement will be read on its own merits.

What a pitch email looks like

Subject: Golden bakery opens second shop April 1, adding 9 jobs

Hi Sam, I saw your piece on new businesses along Washington Avenue. Ridgeline Bakery is opening its second shop at 1200 Washington on April 1 and hiring nine full-time staff. The owner, who started the bakery in her garage, is available for an interview any morning this week. The full release is below and at the link, with a photo you are welcome to use.

[Release text]
[Link to the published release]
[Name, title, phone]

The example is fictional. Note what it does: names the reporter, references their actual coverage, states the news in one line and offers access.

Stay on the right side of the rules

Write to reporters one at a time where you can. Do not buy scraped lists of journalist addresses. If you send pitches in bulk, or the message promotes a product or service, read the FTC's CAN-SPAM Act compliance guide. It covers commercial email and requires, among other things, accurate header information, a subject line that is not deceptive, your valid physical postal address and a working way to opt out. When someone asks to be taken off your list, take them off.

Mistakes that end a local pitch

  • Sending the same message to every reporter at one outlet.
  • A subject line that reads like an ad.
  • A PDF attachment and no text in the email body.
  • Claims that cannot be checked, or adjectives in place of facts.
  • A media contact that nobody monitors.
  • Pitching a story the outlet ran last week from a competitor's angle without saying what is new.

When the outlet says no

Your published release is still a public record of the news, linked from your own site and newsroom. Routed releases are also eligible for full-text republication in network archives such as Press Release For Business, and each destination is listed on the release's public receipt. Local news questions

How do I find the right contact at a local news outlet?

Read the outlet for a week to see who covers your subject, then check its contact or submit-a-news-tip page. TV and radio stations usually route stories through an assignment desk, and events often go to a separate community calendar form.

Should I attach the press release or paste it into the email?

Paste it into the body of the email and include a link to the published version. Many newsrooms filter attachments, and plain text is faster for a reporter to read on a phone.

When is the best time to send a press release to local news?

Early in the working day, so the story can be considered at planning meetings, and with enough lead time for events. Community calendars often have their own deadlines, so check each outlet's submission page.

How many times should I follow up with a reporter?

Once, after a few working days, with something new such as a photo, a date or a detail. If there is still no reply, move on and treat the next announcement as a fresh pitch.

Will a local newspaper publish my press release for free?

It might report on it if the story is genuinely local and newsworthy, but coverage is always the newsroom's decision and is never guaranteed. Paid advertising and sponsored content are separate products that outlets sell through their advertising departments.
