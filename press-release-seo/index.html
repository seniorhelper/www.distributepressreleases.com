What a press release does for search, and what it stopped doing

Written by people who sell press releases, which is exactly why it is worth reading the part where we explain what they will not do for you.

Do press releases help SEO in 2026?

Press releases no longer help SEO through links. Google’s spam policy names them where payment is combined with links that pass ranking credit, so every link in a paid release should carry rel="sponsored" or rel="nofollow". What is left is real but different: a permanent indexed page, entity signals that help systems identify your business, and brand mentions. Treat a release as a brand and entity asset, not a link building tactic.

Reviewed and updated September 27, 2026. We date these pages because the rules in this field change and undated advice ages badly.

Start with the policy, because everything follows from it

Google's spam policy names press releases directly. The specific combination it objects to is payment plus links that pass ranking credit, or links carrying optimized anchor text, in articles, guest posts or press releases distributed on other sites.

The same document says something that gets quoted less often: buying and selling links is a normal part of the web's advertising economy, and it is not a violation as long as the links are qualified with rel="nofollow" or rel="sponsored".

So the rule is not "press releases are bad for SEO." The rule is that a paid placement has to say it is a paid placement. Everything on this site does.

What that means in practice. Every link in every release we publish carries rel="sponsored". Every release carries a visible paid label. If a service is selling you followed links from press releases, they are selling you something Google's own documentation describes as a violation, and the exposure is yours rather than theirs.

What is actually left, and it is not nothing

A permanent indexed page

A stable URL carrying your announcement, your organization details and your entity markup. It stays live, it stays indexed, and it accumulates alongside everything else you publish.

Entity signals

Organization markup connecting your business name to its real profiles helps search systems tell you apart from every other company with a similar name. That disambiguation matters more every year.

Brand mentions

Unlinked references to your company in relevant contexts still contribute to how search systems understand your brand's presence. A mention is not worthless just because it is not a link.

Brand search support

Someone who hears your name and searches it should find current, controlled information. Releases fill that space with material you wrote instead of whatever else is ranking.

Indexing speed

Push protocols shorten the gap between publishing and discovery on participating engines. It affects how fast a page is found, not how well it ranks.

Something to point at

A dated, public, third-party-hosted record of what your company did and when. Useful for credibility long after the SEO conversation is over.

The canonical problem nearly everyone gets wrong

Syndication means the same text exists in several places. Search engines then have to decide which copy is the original, and if you have not told them, they will guess.

The copy on your own newsroom, or the publisher's permanent URL, should be declared the original. Every syndicated reprint should point back to it. Done right, the reprints reinforce the original. Done wrong, they compete with it, and a reprint on a stronger domain can end up outranking the page you actually control.

It takes one line of markup and it is the single most common technical failure in this category. Our schema generator includes the canonical declaration, free, whether or not you buy anything.

Five things that waste a release

  • Keyword stuffing. Repeating a commercial phrase makes the document worse for every reader, human and machine, and does not produce the ranking it is aiming at.
  • Exact-match anchor text. A dominant pattern of commercial anchors is among the clearer manipulation signals, and it is pointless when the links are sponsored anyway.
  • Vague claims. "Significant growth" and "industry-leading" get skipped. A number with a date gets quoted.
  • Burying the news. If the announcement is in paragraph four, most readers and most extraction systems will never reach it.
  • Publishing nothing news-shaped. No amount of distribution rescues an announcement that is not an announcement. Check yours first.

Press releases and SEO

Do press releases help SEO in 2026?

Not in the way they did a decade ago. Links in a paid release should carry rel="sponsored" or rel="nofollow", so they are not a link building tactic. What a release still does is create a permanent indexed page carrying your brand, your entity details and your announcement, which supports brand search and entity understanding rather than raw ranking power.

Are press release backlinks good or bad?

Paid links that pass ranking credit are against Google's spam policy, and press releases are named in that policy alongside advertorials and guest posts. Correctly marked sponsored links are explicitly fine — Google's own documentation says buying and selling links is normal for advertising as long as they are qualified. The fix is disclosure, not avoidance.

What is rel="sponsored" and when do I need it?

It is a link attribute that tells search engines a link was paid for or part of a commercial arrangement. You need it on any link in content you paid to place, including press releases. Google treats these attributes as hints rather than strict directives, but applying them is what keeps a paid placement compliant.

Should the press release on my own site be the canonical version?

Yes. When the same release appears on several sites, the copy on your own domain or your publisher's newsroom should be declared the original and the syndicated copies should point back to it. Otherwise the reprints compete with the original and credit ends up in the wrong place.

Will a press release rank for my keywords?

Rarely, and it is not what they are for. A release ranks best for your own brand name and the specific announcement. Trying to make one rank for a competitive commercial term usually produces a keyword-stuffed document that neither editors nor search engines want.
